Why I built this

I'm a PhD candidate in phage biology, not a career software engineer — but I train seriously, and I kept running into the same wall with fitness apps. Every one of them wanted an account, a subscription, and a firehose of my data going somewhere I couldn't see. I just wanted to log my lifts, progress them sensibly, and own the numbers. So I built the tracker I actually wanted.

The core idea was to do progressive overload properly — not a vague "add weight sometime" nudge, but real program structure across a 12–16 week block that adapts to your goal, level, and equipment. Everything lives in your browser's localStorage. There's no login, no server, no tracking. If you close the tab and come back next week, your history is still there, and it never left your device.

Private by design: no account, no server, no analytics. Your training data stays on your device, and it works even when your phone has no signal in the corner of the gym.

How it works

  1. Take the onboarding quiz

    Seven questions — goal, level, equipment, days per week, cardio preference, and bodyweight — set the shape of your program.

  2. Get your program

    The generator builds a 12–16 week plan with the right split, exercises, and rep schemes for you, or you can hand-build a split yourself.

  3. Train and log

    Open today's session, work through the exercise table, and mark it complete. Progress and benchmarks update as you go.

  4. Own your data

    Everything is saved locally in your browser. Reset, redo the quiz, or switch to the manual builder any time from Settings.

Offline-first & private

Fitness Forge is a Progressive Web App with zero build step and zero dependencies — just vanilla JavaScript, CSS, and a service worker. Add it to your home screen and it behaves like a native app: it opens instantly, works with no connection, and keeps every rep you've logged on your device alone. There's no backend to breach and no account to delete, because there was never one to begin with. For me that's the whole point — a tool I trust with my own training, and one I'm comfortable handing to the people I care about.
